Continuous improvement comes to Microsoft Foundry

Agents on Foundry get better the more you use them. That means you get the right model for the job, while balancing performance, latency, and cost. Foundry: Running and continuously improving agents at scale Once you’ve built your agent, you need a place to run it, govern it, and improve it for all your teams, in every region, alongside hundreds or thousands of other agents.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

New models ship every few weeks, and your customers ask questions no one planned for. That’s continuous improvement, and today at Microsoft Ignite we’re bringing it to Microsoft Foundry. And as part of the new Microsoft IQ , Foundry accomplishes two things that only Microsoft can deliver: your unique intelligence stays your advantage, and your agents keep getting better the longer they run.

Out of the box, an AI model is exactly the same for you as it is for anyone else. Three things help you improve your agents as your teams accomplish real work. Foundry lets you feed agent traces and user input back into the system, creating a continuous learning loop that improves the performance of the system over time.

The system finds and tests improvements; shows you how each one trades off across quality, speed, and cost; and then you decide what ships. Say you want an agent to automate your supply chain processes, and you want that agent to be available in Teams—where your team already works. Just explain what you’re trying to do in clear, everyday language, and GitHub Copilot writes most, if not all, of the code.

And with GitHub Copilot Fusion under the hood, it routes and orchestrates models while you’re building, so you don’t have to. With more than 11,000 models , from OpenAI and Anthropic to Meta, Mistral, open source, our own Microsoft AI (MAI) models, and more, Foundry lets you do just that. And you don’t have to choose just one or be locked into a single model provider.

Foundry’s model router sends simple requests to small, fast models and more complicated asks to larger, state-of-the-art models, so you spend less on tokens, time, and compute. Our testing shows that using Foundry’s model router can save you up to 65% compared with sending everything to the frontier. Entra Agent ID and Foundry guardrails give the agent only the access it needs, and sensitive actions keep a human in the loop as required by your business.

You set the standard, every change is tested against evidence, and your team decides what reaches production. ignite session: The trust foundation… Run agents with the orchestration layer of your choice. Foundry Agent Service runs agents built with your preferred framework and harness through hosted agents , which now handle more concurrent sessions at once (in general availability [GA]) and start faster (in preview).

And for teams that would rather have the harness managed for them, the Microsoft Copilot harness (now in preview) has built-in capabilities like reusable skills and sub-agents. Fashable, an AI-native fashion company, is using voice agents (now in GA) and the Microsoft Copilot harness to make design and sourcing more conversational and agent powered. Agents learn from their own past work and your experts’ feedback, and those lessons become new skills, evaluations, and more.

Continuous improvement in Foundry follows four steps, repeated, so each pass builds on the last: Establish , Identify , Optimize , then Validate and repeat . Establish: You decide what good looks like Improvement starts with a clear standard. In Foundry, that standard lives in evaluations, which are automated tests that score an agent’s responses against criteria you choose.
