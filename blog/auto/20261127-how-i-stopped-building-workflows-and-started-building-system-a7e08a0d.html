How I Stopped Building Workflows and Started Building Systems

The voice provider changes, the CRM changes, the workflow stays. Notice what is not in that list: there is no step that says "the AI handles it." Every step has a rule, a record, and a next step when it fails. I build for what breaks in real life: messy data, dropped calls, missed webhooks.

I kept shipping workflows that worked in the demo and broke on Monday. That is the short version of the last few months. I built a lot of different things — for clients, for businesses we work with, for our own operations, and a few just to see if an idea could become a working product.

And the pattern that kept repeating was the same: the workflow was the easy part. This post is the honest version: the architecture I actually use, one system broken down end to end, what breaks in production, and the four checks I run before I call anything done. The architecture I build everything on Every system I build follows the same five layers.

I did not invent this in a slide deck — it is what the working systems ended up looking like: The point of the layers: you can swap any one of them without rebuilding the system. Most "AI projects" I see are one layer pretending to be a system. The clearest example is a debt recovery system.

The voice agent is the part people ask about. Here is the whole thing: Files come in over FTP and get upserted into Supabase. A scheduler decides what to call and when — do-not-contact rules, permitted hours.

ElevenLabs runs staged scripts, not one long prompt. Payments go through Twilio Pay keypad capture, so the LLM never sees card digits. The same shape shows up in everything else I built in this period: A "Where Is My Order?" support agent Utility and water company solutions for UK businesses A run of proof of concepts and internal systems Different businesses, same five layers, same rule: if a step cannot fail cleanly, the system is not finished.

One study of 85 businesses found 62% of inbound calls go unanswered. It is a system problem — nobody built the capture, the routing, the follow-up and the logging around the phone. A missed call is only a missed call if nothing happens after it.

A name is spelled two ways, an order number is missing a digit, a phone field has a note in it. If your system trusts the input, it breaks on the second day. Validate at the capture layer, not at the AI layer.

An agent inventing a time, forgetting a step, or promising something the business cannot do — that costs you a customer. Staged scripts and guardrails exist for this exact reason. Every system needs a clean point where a human takes over.
