DataGrip and Database Tools User Experience Survey #3 Results

We’re back with the results of our third DataGrip and Database Tools User Experience Survey. The first part of the survey focused on who you are, which tools you use, and what your daily database work looks like. This year, 84.7% of respondents were satisfied or very satisfied with DataGrip and the built-in database tools.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

A big thank you to everyone who took the time to participate! As with our first and second survey , our goal is to understand what works well, what causes frustration, and where we should focus our efforts to improve your experience. In this post, we’ll share the key findings, from overall satisfaction and day-to-day usability to performance, reliability, database support, and AI-powered features.

We’ll also compare the results with previous surveys where meaningful, taking into account differences in the questions and the respondent demographics. This helps us better understand your needs and put the survey findings into context. Software developers and engineers continue to represent the largest group of respondents, accounting for 70% of participants.

Other roles include software architects (6%), team leads and engineering managers (5%), and data engineers (4%). Our respondents are an experienced group: 74% have more than five years of experience in their primary role, and half (50%) have over ten years. When it comes to JetBrains database tools specifically, 37% have been using them for 3–5 years, 25% for 6–10 years, and 8% for more than ten years.

This difference is likely due to how we promoted the survey. This year, we used in-product notifications across all JetBrains products with database features in addition to the other channels. Keep this demographic change in mind when comparing satisfaction results with previous years.

PostgreSQL remains the most widely used database among respondents at 77%, followed by MySQL (42%), SQLite (28%), and Microsoft SQL Server (26%). Other commonly used databases include MariaDB (24%), Oracle Database (23%), Redis (23%), and MongoDB (19%). We also asked respondents which database-related tasks they had performed over the past month.

Here’s what they reported: 95% wrote, edited, or executed SQL queries. 95% browsed databases, schemas, tables, or other database objects. 87% created or configured database connections.

71% created or modified database objects using SQL. Other common activities include importing data (46%), investigating or optimizing query performance (45%), and comparing table data or query results (34%). Most respondents work in relatively small teams: 62% belong to teams of 2–10 people, 15% work in teams of 11–20, and 10% work on their own.

In short, our typical respondent is an experienced developer working in a small team, relying on DataGrip or the built-in database tools in other JetBrains IDEs for a broad range of day-to-day tasks. That’s slightly lower than the 87.5% recorded in our second survey and 90% in the first. However, this year’s survey reached a broader audience through in-product notifications in all JetBrains products with database features.

Why this matters

DataGrip and built-in database tools One of the most notable shifts from the previous survey is how respondents are distributed across our products: 44% use only the built-in database tools in other JetBrains IDEs. It reframes risk for teams that automate sensitive workflows and rely on third-party data flows.

Operational takeaways

  • Software developers and engineers continue to represent the largest group of respondents, accounting for 70% of participants.
  • As in previous surveys, we asked you to rate your experience with different specific aspects of DataGrip and the built-in database tools.
  • Inventory dependent integrations and add regression checks before you widen rollout.
