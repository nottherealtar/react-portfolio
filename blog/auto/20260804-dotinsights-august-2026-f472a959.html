Ever wondered why C# uses the semicolon to terminate a statement? Obviously it comes from the C/C++ heritage it shares with other languages like Java, but where did C get it from? In natural language grammar, the semicolon separates two independent but related clauses; it can also be used to separate items in a list (see what I did there?).

And what is a program but a list of independent but related clauses? (Bonus punctuation trivia: the comma is used to separate items in a list, just like the arguments in a function call. The period is used to end a complete thought, like a sentence or paragraph.
