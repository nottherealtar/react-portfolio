FabCon and SQLCon 2026 in Barcelona: Building the data foundation for Microsoft Copilot and agents

Today, we’re announcing new innovations across Microsoft Fabric and our database portfolio designed to help organizations power their Frontier Transformation, including deep integrations with the new Microsoft Copilot, agentic app development in Microsoft Power BI , end-to-end observability in Fabric, the database hub, and more.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

One of the defining strengths of the Fabric and SQL communities is how openly people share what they are building and learning. This week, nearly 5,000 data professionals are coming together in Barcelona to do exactly that. Across more than 130 sessions, more than 10 tutorials, community meetups, and roundtable discussions, attendees will exchange practical insights and real-world experiences that others can apply in their own organizations.

Microsoft Fabric now supports more than 40,000 customers worldwide, and for decades Microsoft SQL Server has provided the backbone of enterprise workloads including more than 90% of the Fortune 500. Customers like Levi Strauss, H&M, and Thomson Reuters are using these capabilities to unify their data estates, ground AI in their business context, and scale while remaining governed and secure. AI is no longer simply helping developers write code faster.

Agents are beginning to work across the full development lifecycle, from planning and coding to testing and deployment. The opportunity now is to bring that same transformation to the rest of the enterprise. But organizations need more than models and agents.

They need trusted context: governed data, shared business definitions, operational knowledge, and the guardrails that help AI act reliably. This is the foundation of Frontier Transformation: embedding AI into core business processes, grounded in an organization’s unique data, knowledge, and workflows. Microsoft is helping enable this transformation through Microsoft Copilot and Microsoft IQ, which connect AI to an organization’s knowledge, data, applications, and business context.

At the center of this system is Fabric IQ , a shared intelligence layer that connects enterprise data, business metrics, and operational context across the Microsoft ecosystem. By combining unified data from Microsoft OneLake , trusted metrics from Power BI semantic models, and operational context from ontologies and real-time intelligence, Fabric IQ gives people and agents a shared understanding of the business. Powering the new Microsoft Copilot with trusted data from Fabric IQ Millions of Microsoft 365 users rely on Microsoft…

With the introduction of the new Copilot last week , it’s evolving from a conversational assistant into a unified experience where people can assist, delegate, and even build. Home brings Chat and Cowork together, while Code now gives everyone ways to build their own solutions. As Copilot takes on increasingly sophisticated work, access to trusted business context becomes essential.

Today, we are excited to share we are bringing business context from Fabric IQ into Microsoft Copilot, grounding your work in a shared understanding of the business. Microsoft Copilot customers can now tap into AI-powered insights from Fabric as part of their Copilot experience, without additional AI token costs. Fabric IQ in Copilot Chat and Cowork is generally available today, with integration into the new Code experience coming soon through the Frontier program.

For Power BI customers, this semantic foundation is already in place. Business definitions, key metrics, and organizational knowledge captured in trusted Power BI semantic models and reports can now directly inform decisions and actions in Microsoft Copilot. This allows Copilot users to ask business questions and receive AI-powered insights grounded in the semantic data layer their organization already trusts.

Why this matters

Microsoft is helping enable this transformation through Microsoft Copilot and Microsoft IQ, which connect AI to an organization’s knowledge, data, applications, and business context. It reframes risk for teams that automate sensitive workflows and rely on third-party data flows. It is a useful datapoint for how AI-assisted engineering and operations are converging in production.
