TechCrunch Disrupt 2026: Clay’s Kareem Amin on the rise of the GTM engineer

At TechCrunch Disrupt 2026 , Clay co-founder and CEO Kareem Amin will take the AI Stage for “ The GTM Engineer: How AI Created Tech’s Next Big Job Category .” He’ll trace how AI has changed the traditional go-to-market stack and helped create a new discipline in its place, giving attendees a look at what AI-native GTM looks like in practice and how it’s changing company growth.

Two years ago, most companies didn’t have a GTM engineer. Now the role is showing up across tech, with practitioners using AI and automation to build revenue systems that once required multiple people, tools, and manual processes. AI is giving go-to-market teams new ways to research prospects, work with data, personalize outreach, and automate workflows that once moved between separate tools and teams.

As those capabilities converge, companies are rethinking not only their GTM technology, but also who builds and manages the systems behind growth. If you’re building a company or rethinking how your team approaches sales, marketing, and growth, this is a new role worth understanding. Traditional go-to-market work can involve hours of researching accounts, cleaning data, qualifying leads, preparing outreach, and moving information between systems. GTM engineering uses AI, data, and automation to turn more of that work into repeatable systems. Its own GTM engineering guide describes GTM engineers as practitioners who build automated revenue workflows rather than executing individual GTM tasks by hand.

The company says roughly 100 GTM engineering job listings are now appearing each month, including roles at companies such as Cursor, Lovable, and Webflow. For founders and GTM leaders deciding where to put their next dollar or whether to make their next hire, this session offers another way to think about growth. Do you add more people to execute the existing process, or build systems that let the people you already have execute differently?

Get your ticket to Disrupt to understand a role that barely existed a few years ago but could change how your next sales, marketing, or growth team operates. Clay puts AI to work across the GTM stack Clay started in Brooklyn in 2017 with one goal: Give more people access to the power of programming. Its original spreadsheet let users connect information and automate work without writing code.

When salespeople, growth teams, and agencies began using it to find and reach customers, Clay followed them into go-to-market. Today, the company provides infrastructure for GTM teams to access data, run agentic workflows, and launch campaigns. Its OpenAI-powered agent, Claygent, automates research and outreach for sales, marketing, and recruiting teams.

That evolution has been accompanied by rapid growth. TechCrunch reported in February that the company had tripled annual recurring revenue to $100 million in a year and announced an employee tender offer at a $5 billion valuation. Clay has continued growing since then, announcing a $115 million Series D in September at a $7.1 billion valuation.

For companies trying to determine where AI can make a meaningful difference in revenue operations, add Amin’s session to your Disrupt agenda and see how one company is putting that model into practice. Before Clay, he was VP of product at The Wall Street Journal and co-founded collaboration startup Frame, which Sailthru acquired in 2012. Amin started Clay around the idea of putting programming capabilities into more people’s hands.
