Maintainers don’t need more AI bug reports. They need fixes.

Anyone pointing a new model at a project should ask its maintainers first and come with fixes, not just findings. Reports and fixes stayed on GitHub, where maintainers already work. The result: Codename MDASH, using Microsoft’s MAI Cyber model, flagged thousands of possible issues across the six projects.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

AI has made finding potential vulnerabilities fast and cheap. Fixing them still takes human judgment, and in open source, that work falls to maintainers. Many are already flooded with AI-generated security findings, including duplicates and reports that aren’t vulnerabilities at all.

Maintainers are under intense pressure from this flood of reports. As Christian Grobmeier, a maintainer of Log4j, has put it, the AI defending open source has to be better than the AI attacking it . GitHub and Microsoft are using Codename MDASH , Microsoft’s multi-model agentic scanning harness, to find and fix security vulnerabilities in widely used open source software, strengthening the foundation that much of the world’s code is built on.

Our pilot had two goals: send only findings that matter, and never send a maintainer an unverified finding. An AI triage agent from the GitHub Security Lab , built on tailored Copilot skills, removed duplicates, checked validity, and focused on high-impact bugs, so maintainers never had to review raw scan results. Researchers from Microsoft FORGE Lab checked every remaining finding by hand.

Maintainers decided what to fix, how to fix it, and when. After AI triage and hand review by seven Microsoft FORGE Lab researchers, maintainers received 99 private reports. As of November 15, they had fixed 55, and 14 of those fixes were already in a release.

These lessons come from what the six projects fixed and what their maintainers chose to share. Six projects is a small sample, so read them as early lessons. Fixing is where maintainers spend their time.

So every report arrived already checked, with a suggested fix to review, adapt, and merge. Maintainers started from a well-scoped patch, not a raw finding. The first fixes shipped about a week after the initial scan.

Caddy’s maintainers fixed all 22 of the reports they received in a week or less. AutoGPT’s maintainers had recently shipped a new check, and it was broken. The report landed three days later and pointed at the right line.

They took the fix further than the suggested patch, wrote it within a day, and had it in production less than a week later. Finding vulnerabilities is no longer our bottleneck. Fixing them is the hard part, and that depends on maintainers.

Why this matters

Maintainers used the suggested fixes, some as written and some with changes. It reframes risk for teams that automate sensitive workflows and rely on third-party data flows. It is a useful datapoint for how AI-assisted engineering and operations are converging in production.
