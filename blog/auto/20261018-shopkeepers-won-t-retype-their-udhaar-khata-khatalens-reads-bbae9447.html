Shopkeepers won't retype their udhaar khata. KhataLens reads it with Gemma 4, locally

Free ledger apps like Khatabook and OkCredit exist, but they ask the shopkeeper to type every entry into a phone, on top of the notebook he already keeps. He'd be paying to not change his habit: he keeps writing in the notebook, and once a week he photographs the new pages and gets every balance plus a ready-to-send reminder for each customer.

This is a submission for the Hacktoberfest Open-Source AI Challenge Week 2: Side Hustle Walk into almost any kirana shop and you'll find a notebook next to the till: the udhaar khata . A regular customer takes five kilo atta today and pays on Saturday, so the shopkeeper writes it down: date, name, item, amount. Knowing who owes what means re-adding pages of handwriting, with the same customer scattered across many pages.

Then comes the harder part: asking a neighbour for money without it getting awkward. KhataLens starts from the notebook instead: Photograph the pages. Gemma 4 reads every handwritten line into an editable ledger that sits next to the photo.

Corrected rows are tagged, and balances update as you type. The same customer across pages adds up into one balance (udhaar minus jama), sorted by who owes most, with the total to collect always visible. Gemma drafts a short, respectful Hinglish message with the customer's name, the exact ₹ amount, the shop name and, optionally, the shop's UPI ID.

The shopkeeper copies it or opens WhatsApp, picks the contact and taps send himself. Gemma 4 E2B runs locally in Ollama, so the photos, names and amounts never leave it. If the reminders bring back one forgotten ₹500 a month, a ₹99 monthly fee pays for itself.

That price only works because reading a page costs nothing: there's no per-image API bill that grows with every page he photographs. To be upfront: no shopkeeper has used it yet. Everything below was tested on three khata pages I handwrote myself, with made-up names.

KhataLens needs Ollama on the same machine, by design, so there's no hosted demo. Setup is a model pull and two npm commands, all in the README. The photo stays next to the table, and every row is tagged with the page it came from (P1, P2, P3), so the shopkeeper can always go back to the paper: 2.

After all three pages, Ramesh owes ₹240, Pooja ₹150, Imran ₹110 and Arjun ₹40, while Kavita and Sunita are settled: ₹540 to collect (₹1,700 udhaar minus ₹1,160 jama). Pick a customer and a tone, and Gemma writes the message: Photograph a page of a handwritten udhaar khata. Gemma 4 reads it on your laptop, turns it into an editable ledger, totals what each customer owes, and drafts a polite Hinglish payment reminder you can copy or open in WhatsApp.

Nothing is sent automatically and nothing leaves the laptop. Built for Best Use of Gemma 4 at Hacktoberfest 2026 Mumbai.
