Microsoft Ignite 2026: What’s new in Azure, the platform purpose-built for AI

Azure AI core : AI on infrastructure you control. It also gives your teams a path on Azure to build, run, and continuously improve those agents. Ignite session: AI infrastructure from cloud to Edge Azure sets up and tunes the infrastructure for you, so your teams spend less time on configuration and more time moving models and agents into production.

Azure Cobalt 200 : Our highest-performing general-purpose virtual machines (VMs). Azure Catalyst : Agent-led modernization from plan to code. Agentic cloud operations : Agents that keep your cloud running well.

Fabric apps : Governed apps built on trusted data. Over the past year, my conversations with customers have changed. A year ago, the question was which model to choose.

Now it’s how to put agents to work on the processes that run the business, and how to trust them to do it. With the same frontier models available to everyone, the advantage comes from what no one else has: your context. It’s how your business runs, what your people know, and the systems and data you’ve spent years building.

It’s your intellectual property, so it should stay yours, and it should work wherever your people and agents do. Our job is to make every layer beneath your agents better, from the infrastructure that runs them and the estate they act on to the apps they power and the business context that tells them how your company works. Azure is the foundation of Frontier Transformation.

We engineer Azure as one system, so the knowledge an agent needs stays governed and the security you use to protect your people extends to the agents acting for them. And because we’re evolving the open, cloud-native platform you already rely on, you don’t have to start over. As Satya Nadella shared at Microsoft Build in June, we added more datacenter capacity in 18 months than in Azure’s first decade.

Combined with more regions than any other cloud provider, you can scale your AI workloads globally with confidence as demand grows. This week at Microsoft Ignite, we’re sharing what’s new across every layer of the platform. New capabilities spanning compute, storage, and networking let you run AI at scale with the control of your own infrastructure, without having to build, secure, and operate all of it yourself.

Azure Catalyst brings agent-led modernization together in one experience from estate to code. PostgreSQL now has two homes at Microsoft: Azure HorizonDB, generally available for mission-critical scale, and the new Fabric HorizonDB, which connects your PostgreSQL apps to analytics and AI. And Fabric apps, now generally available, let your teams turn trusted enterprise data into working apps.

We’re also introducing Microsoft IQ, the enterprise context management platform that helps you build your own IQ and put it to work in every app and agent. As they take on real work, you should be able to run them on your terms, with your choice of models, your data, and where they run. That control lets you find the right balance of performance and cost for each workload and adapt as models and needs change.
