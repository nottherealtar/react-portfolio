11 games and announcements that got us excited in the latest Galaxies Showcase

Yes, autumn is here, meaning we're teetering on the cavernous hole of near-nothingness as the entire games industry gives GTA 6 the widest berth imaginable. And the latest edition of the Galaxies Showcase has chucked a few more things onto the 'ooooh intriguing!' pile.

The chill in your nostrils and the earthy scent as leaves fall and the sun departs for another year. But that doesn't mean there isn't stuff to be excited about, of course. Actually, quite a lot of things given its nearly two-hour runtime; but while you can watch the full thing in its entirety if you've got the time, we've also picked out a few of the announcements that especially caught our eyes and highlighted them below.

Was all this really just an excuse for Connor to enthuse about penguins? Very promising musical adventure There are no Ghosts at the Grand has a demo I've been very excited about Bristol developer Friday Sundae's There are no Ghosts at the Grand ever since I saw it at a preview last year. It's one of those things that reads like a checklist of my very specific gaming interests, being part musical, part home renovation sim, and part ghostbusting mystery adventure, all unfolding in a dilapidated hotel in a British seaside town.

The idea is you've got 30 days to renovate it (exactly why is part of the mystery given its protagonist has motives so secret, he'll even try hiding them from the player), but there's plenty more to do besides: you can explore the cobblestone streets, go sailing out at sea, or take a scooter into the countryside as you attempt to uncover the truth about the hotel's strange past. By night, there are ghosts to deal with - and throughout all this, the cast is prone to bursting into song. And if that tickles your fancy in the same way…

AfterZero: The Last Drive looks like a frozen Pacific Drive AfterZero: The Last Drive is possibly the first Pacific Drive -like, in that it's a game about driving a car across a post-apocalyptic wasteland - where you'll scavenge, fix up, and upgrade your car, and build out your hideout. It's the work of 3DClouds, the studio mostly known for Formula Legends, but The Last Drive is an altogether different beast. There's a focus on environmental and traversal challenges, similar to the Snow/MudRunner games, or Over the Hill more recently.

Its setting caught my eye, too, unfolding in a post-industrial world where it's always winter. Everything is covered in snow, and the reveal trailer hints there's a mystery at the heart of it all. Like Formula Legends, The Last Drive has a sort of blocky, inky artstyle that elevates its plastic toy-looking cars and objects, and it's releasing sometime in 2027 for PC ( Steam ), PS5, and Xbox Series X/S.

Gritty space job simulator Contact Protocol makes its debut Contact Protocol is an eerie, grim-looking (in a good way) starship job sim that places you in the shoes of a security officer working for a greedy corporation. Your decisions, while surely horrid, are keeping a crew of miners alive as you set course for Jupiter's biggest moon. Aside from a killer premise, Contact Protocol has a wonderful aesthetic, offering a dirty, grisly look - full of delicious retro hardware - at a ruthless life among the stars.

There's a demo available on Steam now , and the full game's out next year. Late Shift is a bizarro world-bender about a missing cinema employee It's hard to explain why, but Late Shift immediately reminded me of Susanna Clarke's wonderful story Piranesi. I think it's to do with the game being about escaping from a world you find yourself trapped in.

Maybe it's also just because there's a section with a white hallway. On closer inspection, Late Shift, from developer AstroCat Studios, is probably more like Remedy's work, presenting players with a bizarre, incoherent world that they have to find their way out of. Specifically, it's a "short found-footage anomaly horror game", according to the Late Shift Steam page , told through the eyes of a missing cinema employee.
