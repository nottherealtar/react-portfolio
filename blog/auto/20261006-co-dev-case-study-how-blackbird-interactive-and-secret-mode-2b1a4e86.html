Co-dev case study: How Blackbird Interactive and Secret Mode helped Escape the Backrooms ride the Backrooms hype wave

A co-dev partnership helped Fancy's hit game Escape The Backrooms reach over 4 million players. But none of these projects have managed to eclipse the popularity of one game in particular: Escape the Backrooms .

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

The Backrooms is a creepypasta Cambrian Explosion of unprecedented scale. Over seven short years, it has ballooned into a shared universe encompassing massive wikis, viral videos, and a movie that just shattered several box office records (like best opening weekend for an original horror movie in history). All of this born from a single, eerie photograph posted on 4chan in 2019.

The Backrooms' influence on gaming has been no less pervasive. It almost single-handedly popularized the aesthetic of liminal spaces, becoming a clear reference point for other hit games like The Exit 8 (which also got its own film last year). On Steam, there are over 270 released games with at least one review that use Backrooms in their title—over 85 of them launching in 2025.

Related: Ubisoft Montreal employees launch collective action to preserve hybrid work schedule With over 4 million players across PC, Xbox, PlayStation, and Switch 2, Escape the Backrooms has become the most popular way to explore this extradimensional, puke-yellow maze of fluorescent lights and musty carpet. It started as a side project of an amateur indie developer and YouTube creator. But, like the creepypasta that inspired it, Escape the Backrooms quickly snowballed into something much bigger.

The solo developer of Escape the Backrooms is an enigmatic person known only by his pseudonym, Fancy. He's an exceptionally rare breed of creator. The type who can somehow juggle school and a part-time job while also running a highly-successful YouTube channel and solo develop a hit video game.

Escape the Backrooms hit Steam Early Access in August of 2022 and peaked just shy of 1,000 concurrent players (CCU). Months prior, Fancy had released a pair of YouTube devlogs that each earned millions of eyeballs apiece. That success compounded significantly over the next two years across three major updates, two seasonal events, and 22 hotfixes.

When Fancy released the Part 4 update in January of 2024, Escape the Backrooms peaked at 16.5K CCU. But then that progress ground to a halt as he set out to port the games to consoles.
