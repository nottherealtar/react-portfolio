Di-Hydro Shows How AI and Digital Twins Can Improve Hydropower Operations

The EU-funded Di-Hydro project is testing how AI, digital twins, forecasting and sensor networks can help hydropower operators make better operational decisions. From fragmented operational data to decision support At the center of the project is the Decision Making Platform (DMP) .

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

Its central tool, the Di-Hydro Decision Making Platform, brings together inflow forecasts, energy-market signals, technical constraints and operational data to support optimized production schedules and scenario analysis. Di-Hydro, short for Digital Maintenance for Sustainable and Flexible Operation of Hydropower Plant, is a Horizon Europe research project with approximately €4.5 million in EU support. It began on October 1, 2023 and is scheduled to run until September 30, 2026.

Rather than presenting AI as a stand-alone feature, the project is building an integrated approach to hydropower operations that includes equipment condition monitoring, environmental sensing, data exchange and real-time visualization. The project matters because hydropower operations involve competing considerations. Operators need to respond to available water, equipment condition, operational limits and energy-market conditions while also considering environmental factors.

Di-Hydro is designed to give teams a more connected view of those inputs, then use AI-enabled forecasting and optimization to support their decisions. According to the project's official overview of the Di-Hydro Decision Making Platform , the platform has been piloted across three use cases in Greece, Italy and Serbia. The pilots involve PPC in Greece, A2A in Italy and EPS in Serbia.

The DMP is a software environment intended to consolidate data that might otherwise sit in separate operational, forecasting and planning processes. It uses AI and forecasting capabilities to generate production schedules while enabling operators to test different scenarios and receive real-time decision support. That approach is significant because a digital twin is more than a visual representation of an asset.

In Di-Hydro, digital-twin concepts connect physical plant conditions, operational information and analytical models. The goal is to help teams simulate potential operating scenarios before acting, rather than relying only on isolated data points or manual coordination. The project's documented components include: Forecasting and optimization based on inflow forecasts, market information and plant constraints.

Digital twins and simulation for evaluating operational scenarios across sites. Predictive maintenance inputs informed by structural health and condition-monitoring sensors. Environmental and biodiversity monitoring , including real-time water-quality sensing.

A secure, interoperable data architecture for sharing information across the project environment. Visualization tools designed to make operational and analytical information easier to interpret. The platform has already been piloted, but Di-Hydro remains an ongoing research and demonstration effort.

Its work is being refined alongside wider digital-twin capabilities, sensor development and deployment guidance. It should not be interpreted as a claim that every hydropower facility can immediately adopt a finished, identical system. It is that forecasting, sensors and simulation can make that expertise more effective when data is connected in a usable workflow.
