Scale reservoir uncertainty analysis with agile reservoir modeling

Agile reservoir modeling is the ensemble workflow engine. The workflow tightly connects the tools reservoir teams already use with Azure-scale infrastructure and the governed data services provided by Azure Data Manager for Energy.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

Turn subsurface uncertainty into decision-ready insight Drilling campaigns are among the most expensive and operationally complex activities in the energy industry. Rigs, crews, equipment, and logistics are scheduled months in advance, leaving little room for delay. Every hour of downtime can carry significant costs.

Geoscientists and engineers rely on reservoir models to understand the subsurface and guide drilling decisions, often under intense time pressure. Yet the current modeling infrastructure that many companies rely on for these critical decisions was designed for smaller datasets, simpler workflows, and siloed operations. As subsurface data grows in scale and complexity, teams need a faster, more connected way to transform uncertainty into actionable decisions.

The combined solution connects ensemble-centric reservoir modeling and simulation with a governed data foundation, elastic Azure high-performance computing , and scalable storage designed for the throughput and concurrency of large ensemble studies. The industry has long recognized the value of ensemble modeling for risk-aware decision-making, but adoption has been constrained by infrastructure and software designed for smaller, sequential studies. Moving from a limited model set to hundreds of realizations multiplies the compute, storage, data movement, and results that must be managed. Connect reservoir workflows to a governed data foundation Building and updating reservoir models draws on many systems: seismic, wellbore, interpretation, and simulation data, plus the applications and compute that process them. When these pieces are fragmented, customers must connect and orchestrate them on their own, and large-scale simulation becomes a disconnected, one-off exercise. SLB and Microsoft combined their respective technologies, ARM engineered by SLB and ADME engineered by Microsoft, to remove that burden.

Rather than a set of loosely connected tools, customers get one integrated workflow in which each component has a clear role: Azure Data Manager for Energy is the governed data foundation. OSDU® Technical Standard-aligned services and OSDU Domain Data Management Services (DDMS) manage seismic, wellbore, interpretation, and simulation data. Petrel™ subsurface software is where engineers define interpretation and uncertainty workflows, connected directly to data in ADME.

It orchestrates the generation of reservoir realizations and simulation runs and supports discovery and analysis at enterprise scale. As a result, teams build ensembles from trusted corporate data and publish results back for discovery and reuse, which reduces data movement and improves collaboration across asset teams. A walkthrough of how this works today: Reservoir engineers define uncertainty workflows in Petrel software and prepare simulation models for supported SLB simulators , including the Intersect ™ reservoir simulator and Eclipse ™ reservoir simulator. ARM generates the realizations, prepares the simulation cases, and coordinates their execution across the Azure environment. Azure high-performance computing executes multiple realizations in parallel.
