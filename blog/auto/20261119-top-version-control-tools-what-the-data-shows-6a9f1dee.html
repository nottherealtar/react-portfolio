Top Version Control Tools: What the Data Shows

GitLab ranks second in both groups, but the gap is much smaller among those hosting professional projects there. Share of developers, weighted survey results Source: JetBrains Developer Ecosystem Survey 2026. For a public project, GitHub’s familiarity can be an advantage.

Key details from the source

Editorial digest: additional context drawn from the original reporting. Open the primary source for quotes, figures, and updates.

It is used by 85.1% of developers for personal projects and by 56.0% for professional projects. That difference is a useful starting point for choosing a platform. A service you use for a side project may also work well for your team.

At work, though, the decision involves more people and more requirements: code reviews, access policies, existing tools, and the way you build and release software. Below, we look at what the survey tells us about those choices, compare the main platforms, and explain where smaller services fit. We also take a separate look at game development, where a repository can contain as much artwork and audio as source code.

Base: developers asked about team tools, n = 4,519. Version control and repository hosting Version control keeps a history of your code, so you can see what changed and go back to an earlier version. A hosting platform such as GitHub or GitLab gives your team a shared place for that code, together with reviews, permissions, and other collaboration tools.

Most platforms covered in this article work with Git, and the survey shows why: 90.7% of developers use Git, while Subversion is down to 5.9%. Why this matters

Over the same period, GitHub’s share for personal projects grew from 83.2% to 86.3%, while GitLab’s numbers stayed within the margin of error. It highlights where brittle integrations become operational debt as vendors iterate quickly. It matters for compliance and go-to-market planning when the rules of the road for tech are still unsettled.
