Jordan Belfort’s new AI voice startup, Tone, wants to sell like the ‘Wolf of Wall Street’

It’s easy to argue the market is already saturated with AI voice startups and technologies, and Tone comes with added risk: Belfort’s co-founder in the business is longtime investor Shervin Pishevar, who has a controversial past of his own. It’s a very unique way of going about building a voice, and it stems from a system of influence and persuasion I’ve been teaching since the late 1980s. One tonality at a time.

Years ago, as questions mounted about WeWork’s soaring valuation, I sat down with co-founder Adam Neumann. We were negotiating a stage interview, and he asked to meet me twice in the weeks leading up to it, once at a WeWork in San Francisco and again at his office in New York. I knew full well that the numbers that SoftBank in particular had assigned to WeWork made little sense , but I came away understanding why investors wanted to throw in their lot with him.

It’s similarly easy to understand why people are enchanted by Jordan Belfort, the self-proclaimed “Wolf of Wall Street.” Belfort famously pleaded guilty in 1999 to securities fraud and money laundering tied to Stratton Oakmont, the Long Island brokerage where salespeople pushed penny stocks in pump-and-dump schemes while Belfort acquired a Southampton mansion, a 167-foot yacht, and at least one helicopter. (He destroyed the latter two while high on quaaludes and cocaine, as depicted in Martin Scorsese’s famous adaptation of…

But Belfort, whose energy has a galvanizing quality, keeps resurfacing, including in recent years as a Miami-based sales consultant and one-time host of crypto workshops at his Miami Beach home. (A New York Times reporter attended a weekend workshop several years ago where the price of admission was one bitcoin or its equivalent value at the time of $40,000.) Now he is moving into the intersection of what he knows best — sales — and the hottest trend on the planet, AI, building out a voice-based AI platform. The secret sauce is that…

Belfort says the platform, called Tone , is already being used by companies that have more sales leads than their human teams can handle. Now, in a move that could also help expand its training data, Tone is launching a self-service platform for content creators and authors who want to turn their books into “expressive AI-generated narration.” Users can select from Tone’s AI voices or, as a voice-cloning feature rolls out, create a clone of their own voice. We also can’t independently verify that the technology works as described…

But if my time with Neumann taught me anything, it’s that doubts about the numbers rarely get in the way of a good pitch. When Belfort looks for investors to back him in this new venture, finding believers won’t be too hard. Here is part of our conversation, edited for length and clarity.

It’s not just what you say, but how you say it. When you’re not in person, body language collapses into tonality. There are about 15 very powerful tonalities that people use all the time.

The phrase I invented for this was “goal-oriented communication.” If I’m trying to be perceived as an expert, I apply tonality strategically. As a non-engineer, I went to first principles: How would I teach a human to do this? I have 30 years of super high-quality data , and I started teaching the model.

It was a breakthrough moment, because it not only sounded exactly like me, it had nailed the tonality. Even the littlest bit of corrupt data dramatically negates the progress you’ve made. How did you and Shervin Pishevar come together?
